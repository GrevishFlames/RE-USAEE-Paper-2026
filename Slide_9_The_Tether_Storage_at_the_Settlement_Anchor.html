Stylized single day, hourly resolution. Load follows a dual-peak shape scaled to the peak slider; solar a clear-sky bell; wind a flat draw at 34% CF. Merit order: must-run baseload at $0 bid, CC gas at 7.4 × gas, steam/peakers at 10.5 × gas, then a scarcity adder that scales with the residual shortfall toward the cap ($5,000 ERCOT SPP analog / $4,000 EPEX DA analog). Storage charges its energy budget in the cheapest hours and offers it back at average charge cost + the spread slider — the opportunity-cost bid. When that offer is the marginal one, the hour clears at the inventory price: decentralized discovery entering centralized formation. Settlement = arithmetic mean over the contract window (ERCOT peak HE7–22 analog · Phelix base 1–24). Screening exhibit — not a production-cost model, not a price forecast.

250 stochastic 60-day expiries. Spot follows a mean-reverting walk at the volatility slider; the front-month carries an expectational premium (OU noise) on top of the unbiased forecast. The tether clamps that premium inside ± (carry ÷ tether strength), where tether strength compounds at-hub inventory coverage with the settlement-coupling regime — cash-only contributes nothing regardless of inventory (the Doug Houseman point: inventory off-settlement is inventory off-curve); opt-in FTS couples weakly; EFP/delivery couples fully. The credit event removes the shocked share of speculative open interest and widens the premium noise; surviving OI = physical base + spec capital retained in proportion to tether strength — calibrated so that an untethered 13× paper market shocked at 90% reproduces the post-Enron outcome: liquidity −90%, never recovered. Gas curves survived 2002 because storage economics kept quoting them. Screening model — not investment advice.

The critique this exhibit answers
"We cannot expect power hubs to behave like Henry Hub or Cushing until we give them the same physical tool oil and gas have always relied on — collocated, grid-scale inventory. And right now, storage is not being built at the hubs." Doug Houseman — paraphrased critique · carried as Point I

The numbers agree with him. Europe's battery fleet already turns over roughly 15× more energy per month than the entire FTS bridge even under generous utilization scenarios — the tether exists on paper across a dozen zones and is load-bearing almost nowhere. Siting is the missing variable: inventory that settles into a distribution feeder or a West Texas node never touches the hub average that futures cash-settle against. Phelix physical-fulfilment products cannot take off while the inventory sits off-anchor.

The formation sequence — how the coupler gets built

Europe is one contract amendment away — the FTS plumbing spans twelve countries and needs only an obligation, plus inventory sited in-zone at scale. North America is one amendment plus one settlement infrastructure away — Nodal and ICE power futures are purely financial; the EFP-at-storage mechanism has to be invented before it can be mandated. Either way the sequence is the same one every commodity has walked: infrastructure first, then the instrument.

Long duration and short duration · unlevered, pre-tax, no ITC

Grid Energy Storage

Eight storage technologies on one engine. Capital and operating cost across development, construction, operation and decommissioning, solved for the capacity payment or arbitrage spread that breaks even and that earns a target return. Duration is the design variable; efficiency and the charging price decide who survives it.

Inputs

The crossover

Installed cost per kW against duration, every technology on one axis. The slope of each line is its energy capex — the marginal cost of one more hour. Lithium starts lowest and climbs fastest because its energy and power capacity are the same hardware. The cavern, reservoir and borehole technologies start high and stay nearly flat, which is the entire long-duration case. Lines are drawn only across each technology’s practical range; the ring marks your current configuration.

Efficiency meets the charging price

Required arbitrage spread as the charging price falls. Efficiency loss costs you the charging price times the energy lost, so when that price goes negative the sign flips and a low round-trip efficiency stops being a penalty. Watch how much steeper the low-efficiency lines are: that slope, not the efficiency number itself, is what a curtailment-driven market pays for.

Where the capital goes

Real cost before escalation to the spend date, for the selected technology and configuration.

What the rate has to cover

Every cost block, discounted and divided by the present value of the revenue quantity being solved for, so each is expressed in the same unit the market quotes. The blocks sum exactly to the breakeven.

Cash flow

Annual free cash flow as bars, cumulative discounted cash flow as the line, both at the price currently entered rather than at the breakeven price.

All eight, side by side

Common power rating, duration and cycling, each clamped to what the technology can actually do — so a 100-hour technology asked for 8 hours is shown at its own minimum, and a 60-cycle technology asked for 150 cycles is shown at 60. Those clamps are the comparison.

What this model does not do

  • No debt, no tax, and no investment tax credit. The 48E credit is worth 30–50% of storage capex and every number here would move materially with it. These are unsubsidised costs.
  • The arbitrage spread is an input, not a dispatch result. There is no hourly price simulation, no co-optimisation against ancillary markets, and no congestion or basis.
  • Degradation is a flat annual rate. Real fade depends on cycling depth, rate and temperature.
  • Interconnection is a single dollar-per-kW input. In CAISO it is a queue position, a cluster study and a number that has moved by an order of magnitude between studies.
  • Natural gas storage is included on a stored-energy basis for comparison only. It does not discharge electricity, so its capacity lens is undefined and its spread is a commodity spread.

On the technology table

  • Lithium and pumped storage carry decades of built cost history. Borehole thermal, GeoTES, iron-air and gravity have roughly one credible datapoint each. The confidence column records that, the comparison chart marks it, and the four low-confidence rows should be read as order-of-magnitude estimates rather than forecasts.
  • Every cell of the technology table is editable in the panel. If you have better numbers than these, the model is built to take them.

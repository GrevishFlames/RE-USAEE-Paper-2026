The Terminal: The Screen and The Page

The two subscriptions every energy desk pays for, rebuilt from the outside with simulated data. The Screen is the exchange front-end: a live order book, an implied engine, a tape, and candles that close on the settle. The Page is the price reporter’s daily assessment sheet: midpoints, ranges, deal counts, and the methodology fine print doing the real work. The third tab says what you are actually paying each of them for — and why, in power, one of the two invoices nearly vanishes.

How to read a gas line
Waha printing negative is not a typo: it is a molecule with no exit paying for disposal — takeaway constraints turning a price into a congestion signal. The common range narrower than the absolute range tells you two lonely deals printed away from the herd. Deal count and BBtu are the units of proof: an assessment on 9 deals is a different epistemic object than one on 94.
The three prices, again
Keep the taxonomy from the research design: traded price (the tape, Tab I), exchange settlement (the candle close), assessment (this page). Physical delivery connects them; nothing makes them identical. The page’s gas midpoint and the screen’s settle can disagree all month — that wedge is the index-vs-settle market, and it trades.

Two invoices, two different products. Neither subscription sells numbers — numbers are cheap. The screen sells a place where anonymous orders can rest; the page sells a methodology that turns scattered bilateral deals into a benchmark. Decentralized price discovery, centralized price formation — one aphorism, two business models.

The power anomaly — the conference point
Run the two invoices through power and one nearly vanishes. The ISO is simultaneously the venue, the clearinghouse, the settlement formula, and the free publisher of the prices — so the assessment layer survives only at the bilateral margins the ISO doesn’t touch. There is no independent physical benchmark for a PRA to assess, because there is no physical market outside the ISO’s optimization to observe. That is the same finding as the missing index-vs-settle market and the missing delivery seat, seen from the subscription desk: power collapsed discovery, formation, settlement, and publication into one institution. Storage-backed physical settlement is what re-separates them — it creates transactions outside the optimization worth assessing, and therefore, for the first time, something in power for a price reporter to independently report. When the exhibit audience asks "so what would change?", the answer is: this page grows a real power section.

First, the corrections — say these right
"ICE Energy OTC" → just ICE now
That was the original 2000-era identity — the OTC energy platform that grew up beside NYMEX. In 2012 ICE converted its cleared OTC energy swaps into futures (Dodd-Frank arbitrage), so today the bilateral and futures markets live on one platform. Say "the ICE energy complex"; saying "ICE OTC" dates you to 2011.
"ICEBlock" → ICE Block, a reporting tool
Not a venue: it’s the facility for reporting privately negotiated block trades into the exchange for clearing — the regulated descendant of the broker phone deal. The trade happens off-screen; ICE Block is how it becomes a cleared position. Adjacent: ICE eConfirm for OTC confirmations.
"WebICE" → still right, one nuance
WebICE remains the execution front-end, launched from the ICE Launcher alongside ICE Chat (the ex-YellowJacket IM where broker markets stream with quote-recognition) and ICE Options Analytics. The data/charting terminal is a separate product, ICE Connect. Execution and analytics are two subscriptions, not one.
"WoodMac’s Genscape" → correct, with a date
Genscape was acquired in 2019 (Verisk, folded into Wood Mackenzie; WoodMac itself was later sold to Veritas). The sensor network survives inside WoodMac’s short-term analytics and PowerRT; older traders still say "Genscape" for the monitors the way they say "Kleenex."

The stack, read as your thesis
Gas and oil needed four paid layers because discovery, formation, settlement, and observation are separate businesses run by separate parties. Power’s stack is top-light and bottom-heavy: the ISO absorbed the venue, the formula, and the publication, and the free layer (OASIS, EQR) does work that costs five figures a seat elsewhere. What survives commercially in power is exactly what the ISO doesn’t produce: bilateral memory (Megawatt Daily), congestion tooling (Yes Energy), and physical observability (PowerRT). A storage-backed physically-settled instrument would regrow the missing middle layers — a real formation window, an independent assessment, a delivery seat to observe. The stack itself is your exhibit: hold it up and ask why power’s version has holes in it.

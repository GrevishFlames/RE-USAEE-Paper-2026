Why futures, and not swaps — the counterparty argument
These instruments do not compete with VPPAs, index swaps, or bespoke OTC structures — those remain the right tools for shaped, nodal, long-horizon procurement. They serve a counterparty those structures are mechanically unable to hold: the inventory operator, whose exposure did not exist before grid-scale storage and whose book has three properties only an exchange-traded contract satisfies.
Fungible

A wind farm’s exposure is shaped, nodal, and volumetric — which is why it goes bespoke. An inventory operator’s exposure is a quantity, at a hub, over a period. That is the standard contract. Storage is the first power participant whose position needs no customizing.

Rollable

A PPA hedge is set once against a static obligation. State-of-charge moves every day — and the hedge ratio moves with it. You cannot rebalance a VPPA. A continuously managed inventory book requires a cleared, fungible, exit-at-will instrument; the swap cannot mechanically hold the position.

Financeable

A merchant borrowing against stored energy needs a hedge the financing desk will accept as collateral support. A bilateral swap drags counterparty credit into the collateral package; a cleared future does not. Cleared instruments are the precondition for the inventory-financing class power currently lacks.
